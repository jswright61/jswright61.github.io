NPR is doing a series of podcasts and stories on Deadheads: [Legacy and Future of the Grateful Dead fandom](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/27/nx-s1-5897507/how-did-you-become-a-deadhead)

I wrote a script and recorded a voice memo and submitted it to them. I doubt they'll use it, but I had fun writing it and recording it. I'm sharing it here:

> I’m Scott, from Atlanta, Georgia.
>
> I became a Deadhead in St. Louis in the late 1970s. I knew the hits, but the Grateful Dead weren’t one of my top 10 bands.
>
> Then I heard a local cover band, Jake’s Leg, play “Not Fade Away” into “Goin’ Down the Road Feeling Bad.”
>
> Hearing those songs flow together, almost becoming one piece of music, changed something for me. That was the hook, the transitions: always a little different, always fascinating, and almost always seamless.
>
> I saw the Dead 23 times before Jerry's death in 1995, without ever seeing a bad show or hearing the same set list twice.
>
> I also got into tape trading. We exchanged lists and mailed recordings, but what I remember most was the generosity. If you asked kindly and covered postage, someone might send you a show just to keep the music moving. We called that “turning the wheel” — an early version of paying it forward.
>
> For Christmas 2024, my wife, definitely not a Deadhead, gave me tickets to see Dead & Company at the Sphere. We went in May 25. She says she could have died peacefully without ever experiencing “Drums and Space,” but she had a great time. We still cherish the memory, and wear the swag.
>
> I don’t know where this music goes next. But it has always found a way to move from one song, one show, and one generation into the next.
>
> When it finds its next transition, I’ll be there.
